# Everyday walking tools

The **Map** screen has a compact route summary with **Start walk**. Use **Compare routes** to see travel time, mapped roof cover and access checks. Tap **Minimise** to make more room for the map. **Nearby** finds mapped toilets, seats and clinics. **Aa** sets the app language, text size, contrast and motion preference. When a walk starts, the **Walk** section shows the route cue and pause or resume action above its expandable controls.

**Reports** shows your submissions on this browser. In the server version, report history displays the dates for Reported, Checked and Fixed when those stages occur. Report photos are optional and kept on the device. After finishing a walk, **Share a local shortcut** opens a draft for editing and review. A recorded trace can be trimmed before submission. GitHub Pages saves reports and shortcut drafts on the device.

## The eight features

1. **Time-budget return walks.** Explore offers 15- and 30-minute budgets; Plan also supports return walks with the regular budget controls. A single search covers both legs, so the turning point never resets the continuous-walking/rest clock. Crossing waits, physical rest detours and rest duration count toward the budget. Routes prefer an alternative return path where feasible, but may retrace paths. The estimate can be shorter than the selected maximum. Turning points are network locations, not invented facilities or verified destination entrances.
2. **Condition timeline.** “Along your walk” follows actual ordered route traversals, including split edges, crossing waits, rest detours and arrival. It distinguishes unknown roof cover from recorded absence, shows mapped slope and crossing checks, and lets the walker focus the map on a section.
3. **Facility-first search.** Explore searches mapped seats, toilets, drinking water and shelters through the pedestrian network. The same walking requirements and total budget apply. Closed/unavailable facilities and explicit access failures are excluded. Missing evidence stays visible; no facilities are fabricated for the public pilot.
4. **Post-walk comfort feedback.** Finished walks accept optional heat, effort and access ratings plus a note. Feedback is saved with actual active time and optional recorded GPS distance. Personal impressions never become a field verification.
5. **Personal pace suggestions.** Two completed walks in the same profile need at least 100 metres and two minutes of usable moving GPS data each. Low-accuracy fixes, stale/duplicate timestamps, jumps, long gaps and pauses are excluded. The median of up to ten eligible walk speeds is suggested; applying it is explicit. A planned distance or timer-only walk cannot calibrate pace. Changing walking profile restores its profile estimate.
6. **Offline walk packs.** Save captures the route, ordered instructions, nearby mapped facilities, requirements and dated condition records on this device. A small service worker caches only the standalone offline viewer. Offline navigation to the planner falls back to that viewer; it does not claim the entire planner, live weather or new routing works offline. A downloadable self-contained HTML pack independently displays its route sketch, instructions and conditions without network resources. OSM street tiles are never bulk-downloaded or cached by this worker. Saved ratings are snapshots, and offline views ask the walker to recheck conditions.
7. **Quick condition reports.** A walker chooses a specific path/crossing or mapped seat and records a blockage, failed light or unavailable seat with a timestamp and optional note. Reports are stored on the device. In the server version, sharing is explicit through `/api/condition-updates`, which fixes status to `reported` and does not accept client-supplied auditor/status fields. Retry retains the original observation time. Reports become overdue after 24 hours; they do not automatically reopen a path or reveal an older positive observation. GitHub Pages supports personal reports only, with no shared writes.
8. **Consistent mobile navigation.** Explore, Plan, Walk and Saved remain reachable during a recording. The recording uses a captured plan, so planning another trip or receiving an update does not silently change the route being recorded. Finish the current walk before starting another.

## Recording and device storage

Starting a walk starts an active-time timer. GPS recording is separately enabled by the walker and may be denied without breaking the timer. Pausing clears the GPS anchor so movement during the pause is excluded. Hiding the page pauses recording; no reliable background tracking is promised. A best-effort checkpoint is written about every 15 seconds and on pauses. Reloading restores an unfinished walk in a paused state. Completed walks and feedback appear in Saved, where individual items can be removed.

Packs, location traces, feedback and pace history remain in this browser, with explicit downloads for portability. There is no account sync or automatic transmission of tracks. Shared condition reports are sent only by the explicit share action. Storage failures are surfaced; a failed save is not reported as successful. Clearing browser data removes the local collection. Collections allow up to 20 packs, 100 completed walks and 100 personal reports; users can remove older items to free space.

The PostgreSQL/PostGIS schema is unchanged. The full server's existing verification workspace is a trusted prototype and still needs authenticated contributor/moderator roles before a public multi-user launch. No paid provider or supplementary hosting service is introduced.

## Validation

Unit coverage includes return-trip budgets, a rest interval spanning the turning point, ordered traversal data, facility availability, source uncertainty, GPS quality filters, profile-specific pace eligibility, overdue negative reports, storage errors, and HTML escaping. API integration checks exercise reporting with real PostgreSQL/PostGIS and reject unsupported targets/status escalation. Browser validation exercises the public export, GPS opt-in/pause/resume, feedback and history reload, pace application, report persistence, fully offline navigation, portable HTML, isolated facility fixtures, and small-screen large-text controls.
